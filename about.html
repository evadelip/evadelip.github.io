Profile

I am Evangelia (Eva) Deliporanidou, a PhD candidate in Astrophysics at the University of Cambridge. My work focuses on modelling solar and stellar irradiance with statistical methods, machine learning, and physically informed models.

I enjoy connecting scientific rigor with computational efficiency, especially when building models that are interpretable, uncertainty-aware, and predictive under complex conditions.

I am also highly interested in quantitative finance, especially where statistical learning, risk modelling, and decision-making under uncertainty intersect with real-world markets.

Education

University of Cambridge · PhD in Astrophysics (Oct 2023 - Present)

  • Supervised by Prof. Giulio Del Zanna in the Astrophysics Group, Department of Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Physics (DAMTP). Funded by an STFC (UKRI) Doctoral Studentship.
  • PhD research focuses on developing physically motivated models of solar and stellar EUV irradiances by combining differential emission measure modelling, atomic physics, and proxies of activity. The resulting models enable reconstruction of stellar spectra for stars where direct EUV observations are unavailable.
  • Research collaborations with the University of Cambridge, NASA Goddard Space Flight Centre, NASA's Laboratory for Atmospheric and Space Physics (LASP), the international CHIANTI collaboration, and the ESA F-class mission SIRIUS, contributing to development and validation of stellar XUV irradiance models.
  • Contributed to the ongoing development of the CHIANTI atomic database (https://www.chiantidatabase.org), an internationally recognised collaboration honoured with the 2024 NASA Group Achievement Award for contributions to astrophysics. CHIANTI is widely used in solar physics to analyse spectroscopic observations (over 5,200 citations).
  • Extensive scientific programming in Python, IDL, and Fortran for spectral synthesis, plasma diagnostics, and astrophysical modelling.
  • Expected thesis submission: March 2027.
